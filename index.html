The internet isn't going anywhere. Neither are we.

Students spend hours a day online — telling them to simply use it less misses the point. Our mission is to make every one of those hours safer and sharper: browsing securely, protecting personal privacy, recognizing online dangers before they escalate, managing feeds intentionally, and questioning what shows up on the screen instead of absorbing it — with practical moves they can make on their own phones the same afternoon.

1

Privacy that sticks

Which settings matter, who really sees your posts, and a 5-minute account lockdown.

2

Spotting fakes

Reverse-image search, doctored screenshots, and the tells of a made-up story.

3

Asking who benefits

Who made this, why, and what it wants from you — before you like, share, or believe.

4

Owning the feed

Training the algorithm on purpose so it works for you — not on you.

The presentation

One session. Seven skills.

45 minutes, fitted to your bell schedule, in person across DFW or virtual anywhere. Discussion-driven, with live examples from the platforms your students are on right now.

Questions principals ask us

Is it really free?

Yes. No invoice, no sponsorship pitch, no data collection. We're students doing this because we think it matters.

What grades is it for?

Middle and high school. We adjust the examples and pacing — 6th graders and juniors get different versions of the same talk.

Assembly or classroom?

Either. We've done auditoriums and single classrooms; smaller groups get more discussion, bigger ones more demos.

Do teachers need to prepare anything?

Just a projector and 45 minutes. We bring the slides, the examples, and the discussion prompts.

Can you present virtually?

Yes — anywhere. In-person visits are limited to the DFW area around our own school days.

Is the content vetted?

Our syllabus is public — request the one-pager above — and a faculty advisor reviews everything we present.
